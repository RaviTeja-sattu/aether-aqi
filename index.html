Reconstructing India's air, hour by hour.

From a single daily satellite snapshot, AETHER-AQI reconstructs nationwide hourly (24×7) Surface AQI across all six CPCB-regulated pollutants — PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, CO, O3 — using a regime-adaptive, physics-informed ensemble that learns region- and season-specific atmospheric behaviour, validated against real CPCB ground truth under both chronological and spatial holdouts.

How it works

One satellite pass, six stages, a full day.

India's afternoon TROPOMI overpass gives one trustworthy reading a day. Everything downstream exists to turn that single hour-13 anchor into a physically consistent 24-hour picture.

01

Data & feature engineering

Two merged CPCB × ERA5 master tables — 3.0M rows / 478 stations (2013–2025) and a 1.49M-row / 477-station TROPOMI-joined table (2019–2025) — feed a 43–55 column feature set per pollutant: log transforms, harmonic time encodings, wind/humidity terms, and the hour-13 satellite anchor with a boundary-layer-height (BLH) physics baseline.

02

Regime-adaptive clustering

Every station is assigned to one of 7 pre-trained atmospheric regimes using six criteria — environmental characteristics, Köppen climatic zone, elevation, season, population density, and meteorology — so a coastal city and a Himalayan town are never forced through the same physics.

03

Hourly ML ensemble

138 trained per-hour models (LightGBM, HistGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, Extra Trees) — a "winners ensemble" keeping whichever algorithm wins each (pollutant, hour) slot — refined further by bias recalibration and targeted retraining of the 36 weakest slots.

04

Physics-based diurnal equations

Seven fitting methods compared per cluster against each regime's 24-point median profile — Fourier series, pollutant-specific NLS physics forms, seasonal stratification, per-station averaging — encoding the well-known rise and fall of pollution across a real day.

05

Regime-adaptive fusion

A LightGBM meta-model predicts a blend weight α across 4,464 (pollutant, cluster, season, hour) groups. Final Prediction = α·ML + (1−α)·Physics — chosen production framework "F5" out of five candidates benchmarked head-to-head.

06

AQI computation & validation

Fused concentrations run through CPCB's National AQI breakpoint tables. Checked two ways — chronological holdout and a 15% spatial holdout of 71 stations never seen in training — with spatial R² (0.896) edging out temporal R² (0.872).

Why the hybrid wins

The blend never costs accuracy — and often buys a lot of it.

F5 (the regime-adaptive blend) matches or beats raw ML on every pollutant, on both holdouts, while comfortably beating physics-only everywhere.

Ozone is where the hybrid pays off most: physics-only nearly fails on unseen stations, but the regime blend recovers essentially all of the ML model's accuracy by learning to down-weight physics specifically for Ozone. PM2.5/PM10 defer almost entirely to ML (learned α ≈ 94%) because the meta-model figured out physics adds little there — without being told to. Because Objective 1's core mission is predicting AQI where no monitor exists, F5 was selected as the production framework specifically because its accuracy holds on unseen locations rather than being inflated by station familiarity.
